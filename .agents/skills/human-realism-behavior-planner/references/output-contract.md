# Output contract

## A. Structured JSON

Use the keys and enums in scene-spec.schema.json. The root contains:

- schema_version = "1.13" and a stable scene_id.
- references: every inspected image, its path or user-provided handle, concise observation, and confidence.
- input_plan: the exact combination of first, last, intermediate, and semantic-reference inputs; confirmed duration when known; ordered timeline anchors; semantic role and target assignments; explicit dual-role declarations; resolved role conflicts; loop intent; and consecutive interpolation segments covering the whole clip.
- control_requirements: every shot-critical invariant, its importance, preferred enforcement channel, supporting evidence, and fallback. Distinguish prompt guidance from image anchors, generation settings, deterministic post-processing, hybrid control, and unsupported requirements.
- subjects: one entry per person, with the image IDs supporting identity, region-specific identity anchors, reference view coverage, an unseen-region policy, and explicit appearance changes.
- intent: the user's request, setting/camera/duration when supplied, initial state, ordered beats, and final state. Unknown optional scene settings are null.
- realism_selection: one profile per subject. State the subject state and realism mode; explicitly classify respiration, blinking, ocular fixation, facial tonus, and postural micro-adjustment; and define bounded variation for repeated action.
- behavior_plan: one plan per subject. Begin with performance control: behavioral reading, support topology, posture preservation, primary-action observability, semantic-activation control, facial-state control, independent temporal layers, concurrency, bounded variability, and loop handling. For the primary action, state driver visibility, completion criterion, visible distinguishing cues, confusable substitutions, causal order, endpoint evidence, fallback, prompt-activation risk, and safe prompt strategy. Record risky social, affective, cinematic, or spatial terms; their implied behaviors; the exact intended visible meaning; a prompt-safe description; and held channels. Separately govern expression category, gaze, eyelids, brows, cheeks, lips, jaw, throat, and head pose. Diagnostic evidence remains internal when direct exposure would amplify motion, localize anatomy, violate posture, or change the behavioral reading. Cover gaze, face, head, gesture, posture, locomotion, and speech with applicable phase synchronization; describe Body/Space/Shape/Effort for every subject beat; represent visible facial expression changes through timed Action Units and combinations; explicitly decide five extended physiology cues; inventory relevant passive elements; record physiology couplings and one typed causal dynamics link per secondary track, with acyclic propagation.
- spatial_plan: one camera track, one composition-control plan, and one spatial track per subject. Separate camera and subject trajectories; record path, depth, orientation, framing, subject scale, crop, background registration, visible boundaries, off-frame regions, support, and reference coverage. Include a motion envelope for every action-critical moving element, with its active windows, full screen-space path, edge clearance, boundary risk, visibility requirement, overall fit, and failure policy. Structure human-object, human-human, and human-environment interactions around affordance, approach, contact, manipulation, release, and reaction. Record occlusion windows and reappearance constraints.
- scene_physics: scene-wide gravity assumption, known objects with qualitative mass and mobility, force fields, support and object contacts with qualitative friction, environmental drivers, and typed visible dependencies such as shadow, reflection, occlusion, and perspective changes. Use unknown rather than inventing measurements.
- motion_sources: source clips used for motion or timing, with role, duration, observations, exclusions, and confidence.
- motion_schedule: one normalized temporal plan per subject. Map every subject beat to an ordered primary phase; separate active travel and dwell; preserve tempo and velocity shape; define articulated kinematic chains, contact, counterbalance, and release; schedule vitality and driven secondary response; and state descending priority, coordination, and anti-synchrony rules.
- intent_resolutions: material omissions, ambiguities, contradictions, physical impossibilities, and reference conflicts that were corrected, assumed, retained as intentional stylization, or left unresolved.
- global_rules: selected G01–G29 rules with scene-specific constraints.
- components: selected Level 2 components, each with Template B's observation, intent, preservation, primary motion, secondary motion, deformation/interaction, continuity, negative constraints, and traceability. Template B's acceptance/evaluation section is intentionally absent.
- assumptions and unresolved_conflicts: explicit limits of the evidence and decisions.

Use exact Level 2 component IDs such as "03.02", not invented identifiers. The component name should match the source heading. Put observed reference details only in reference_observation; put planned changes and plausible unseen behavior in the other fields or assumptions. A component can be active, visible-static, occluded, outside-frame, n/a, or uncertain. Include occluded/outside-frame components only when they causally matter to the visible scene. A visible expression change needs an anatomically defensible AU plan with onset ≤ apex ≤ offset; a held neutral or hidden face does not need invented AUs.

For a stationary component, set primary_motion.required to "no", give a short stillness description, and keep timing null. Secondary motion may independently be "no" or "conditional". Stationary primary action does not automatically suppress baseline physiology.

Give every baseline cue one of these explicit statuses: required, conditional, suppressed, not_visible, not_applicable, or uncertain. A required or conditional cue needs a scene-specific rationale, visible effects, timing strategy, variability, `G16`, and at least one supporting Level 2 component with active primary or secondary motion. Suppression needs a physical, narrative, or intentional-style rationale. Hair, fabric, eyelashes, and soft tissue move only as responses to identified drivers; do not add uncaused ambient motion.

For repeated natural human action, set repeated_action to true and vary at least two bounded dimensions, such as timing, amplitude, trajectory, acceleration, inter-region phase, or settling. Use exact repetition only when mechanically exact or intentionally nonrealistic behavior is part of the request.

Motion schedule windows use normalized clip time: `0.0` is the first generated frame and `1.0` is the end. Use broad event windows for sparse biological actions rather than exact instants. A count range may select fewer events than the number of candidate windows; event duration uses physical seconds because it should not scale with clip length. Repeated primary phases share a `repetition_group`; after the first instance, each phase states which approved dimensions differ and how. Secondary tracks must name their driver, response, lag, damping, and settling. Protected windows lower competing motion so a required subtle cue remains legible without freezing the subject. The priority order lists `primary_action`, all active cues, and all secondary tracks once in descending preservation order.

Every supplied image must appear in `input_plan` as a timeline anchor, a semantic reference, or both. Timeline anchors use normalized time even when the user provides seconds. First and last anchors are fixed at 0.0 and 1.0; intermediate anchors are strictly inside that interval and have unique times. Keep anchors in ascending order. Semantic roles are evidence-bounded: for example, a face-identity reference does not define body shape, clothing, pose, framing, or timeline state. Target every subject-related role to a subject and every object role to a known object. Put any intentional anchor-plus-semantic overlap in `dual_role_reference_ids`.

Segments must cover `[0.0, 1.0]` consecutively without gaps or overlaps. Segment boundaries must match their cited anchors; use null only for an unconstrained generated beginning or ending. Each segment describes how the body, camera, contacts, objects, expression, and passive dynamics transition between its boundary states. `requires_morph` and `uncertain` feasibility require a recorded user decision, assumption, or unresolved conflict. Do not silently force physically incompatible frames into a naturalistic transition.

When a destination duration is confirmed, `scripts/resolve_motion_schedule.py` converts input-anchor and segment timing, normalized motion, behavior-channel and phase, facial-action, camera, occlusion, force-field, and environmental-driver timing into seconds while preserving the normalized source, semantic assignments, candidate windows, event-duration bounds, count ranges, priority, variation notes, coordination rules, subject tracks, interactions, and scene contacts. It is a derived artifact rather than a fourth canonical output.

Keep arrays empty where the category has no applicable constraint. Use null for unknown scalar details. Reference IDs in every component and subject must exist in references. Subject IDs in beats and components must exist in subjects. Every global rule ID must exist in the standard. A traceable component should cite at least one relevant global rule.

## B. Human-readable specification

Use the same scene ID, input configuration, timeline anchors, semantic references, interpolation segments, loop intent, subjects, references, identity anchors, action beats, realism decisions, behavior channels, movement quality, facial actions, spatial tracks, interactions, occlusion events, causal dynamics, scene contacts, motion schedule, intent resolutions, selected component IDs, preservation constraints, assumptions, and unresolved conflicts as A. For restrained performance, make the selected amplitude, motion budget, held regions, quiet-region guards, and prohibited human-motion substitutions easy to review. Organize for quick human review; group low-impact components when doing so remains unambiguous. State uncertainty plainly. Do not add facts or actions absent from A.

## C. Model-neutral generation brief

Write a model-neutral generation brief as a single concise positive prompt. Identify each image by its assigned timeline or semantic role. Describe anchor-to-anchor transitions chronologically without presenting semantic references as frames. Compile identity anchors, composition control, camera and subject trajectories, interactions, occlusion and reappearance, behavior channels, movement quality, visible facial actions, contact and environment dependencies, primary phases, priority, and coordination into natural language. Preserve the primary action according to its selected prompt strategy: expose direct evidence only at low risk, otherwise describe a holistic visible outcome, reclassify, ask, or accept bounded ambiguity. The detailed causal chain remains internal when naming it would create extra motion or a different performance category. Preserve normalized ordering and relative windows; convert to exact time only when the destination duration is known. Name explicit temporal layers, mention compact layers once, and leave implicit or omitted layers unnamed. Hidden support and off-frame anatomy remain planning constraints rather than prompt content unless the shot reveals them. Prefer positive visible invariants over catalogs of prohibited motion. Keep the requested primary action dominant and preserve held visible regions. Prioritize constraints that materially affect this shot. Do not add model-specific syntax, dump JSON keys, AU codes, or every anatomical rule. Ask a blocking clarification before delivering a brief that would otherwise choose the user's intent silently.

Schema 1.13 intentionally rejects earlier specifications. Regenerate or migrate them so image roles, anchor timing, semantic targets, interpolation segments, control enforcement, identity and viewpoint coverage, camera and subject trajectories, composition authority, visibility boundaries, action-critical motion envelopes, safe edge clearance, prompt exposure, primary-action observability, activation risk, safe prompt strategy, semantic-bundle risk, facial-state authority, confusable substitutions, structured interaction, support topology, posture preservation, behavioral intent, independent temporal layers, concurrency, bounded variability, loop handling, channel coverage, movement quality, facial timing, scene causality, motion-source authority, real-time tempo, articulation, contact, and vitality decisions cannot remain implicit.

No artifact includes a quality assurance checklist, pass/fail status, generated-video evaluation, or post-generation instructions.
