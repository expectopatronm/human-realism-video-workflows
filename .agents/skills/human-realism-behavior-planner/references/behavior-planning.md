# Model-neutral human behavior planning

Build `behavior_plan` after interpreting intent and before the motion schedule. It describes what each visible system does and why; `motion_schedule` places those decisions in time. Build `scene_physics` once for the scene. The [human motion video generation survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03883) distinguishes input understanding, motion planning, generation, refinement, and output; this skill owns the first two phases. These are planning constraints, not claims that a video model can simulate tissue or solve physics numerically.

## Clarification gate

Ask the user a short, concrete question when different plausible answers would materially change identity, the main action, emotional meaning, contact, framing, duration-sensitive timing, or the final state. Ask for an inaccessible reference image or missing action before planning. Also ask for an additional view, or ask permission to constrain the shot, when a planned turn, camera move, interaction, or occlusion would reveal identity-critical regions that the references do not support. Group dependent questions and show a conservative default only when that default is genuinely reversible. Continue independent analysis while awaiting an answer. Do not produce a final generation prompt that silently resolves a blocking ambiguity.

With multiple images, first establish whether each one is a fixed first, intermediate, or last state or an unpinned semantic reference. Ask when folder placement, prose, or duplicated files disagree; when an intermediate time or semantic target is missing; or when adjacent anchors require incompatible identity, anatomy, contact, object, camera, or scene states. Do not infer a timeline from filenames or treat alternate identity views as action keyframes. See [multi-image-conditioning.md](multi-image-conditioning.md) for the complete input-role gate.

For ordinary unspecified details, choose the smallest naturalistic behavior supported by the image and intent, mark its evidence as inferred, and record a material assumption or `intent_resolution`. Questions should concern creative choices the user can answer, not ask them to supply anatomical or physics expertise. Never infer a visible fact from an occluded region.

## Behavior channels

Give each subject one explicit disposition for gaze, face, head, gesture, posture, locomotion, and speech. `active` means behavior changes; `held` means a meaningful pose or target is maintained; `not_visible`, `not_applicable`, and `unspecified` explain why no behavior is planned. Link active and held channels to beat IDs and describe their coordination in `sync_with` and `coordination_rules`. For active gestures and head acts, add only the applicable preparation, meaningful stroke, hold, relaxation, and return phases. Use `sync_points` when a specific phase must precede, overlap, or follow another channel's phase. Do not force a wave's phase vocabulary onto breath, gaze, or a static pose. Let a gaze shift precede a reach or a grip precede lifting when the causal order demands it.

## Movement quality

For every subject beat, describe Body (participating regions and initiator), Space (direction, pathway, and range), Shape (changing body form or relationship to a target), and Effort (weight, spatial focus, time, flow). Use `neutral` or `unspecified` when the image and request do not justify a quality. These labels describe observable or intended motion; they are not personality or emotion diagnoses. Keep the movement quality consistent with the schedule's kinematic chain, tempo, velocity profile, and repetition variation.

## Facial action

Separate gaze, blink, head pose, and expression. If a visible expression changes, specify the facial Action Units that have a defensible visible effect, with laterality, bounded intensity, and normalized onset, apex, and offset. Link each AU to the beat it expresses and record combinations when two or more units act together. Coordinate brows, eyelids, cheeks, lips, and jaw without creating asymmetry or emotion the user did not request. A held neutral expression can have an empty AU list while facial tonus remains represented in baseline cues. A hidden face has no visible AUs. Treat AUs as a planning vocabulary; the FACS manual's formal coding criteria require trained observation and are not established by an inferred image-to-video prompt.

## Physiology and passive dynamics

Keep the five baseline vitality cues in `realism_selection`. Explicitly decide whether swallowing, exertion, fatigue, autonomic response, and speech–breath coupling are required, conditional, suppressed, hidden, inapplicable, or uncertain. Active extended cues need a cause, visible effect, and beat-relative timing. Add `physiology_couplings` only when one system actually affects another in the shot: speech and breath, exertion and breath/posture, gaze and blink, or a requested swallow or fatigue response. Do not add swallowing, fatigue, or autonomic signs as generic decoration. A blink may coordinate with a head or gaze change when warranted; avoid a rigid rule that every head turn triggers one. Inventory visible, relevant passive elements in `passive_elements`; mark each as affected with its dynamics track, held with a reason, or not visible. Connect each material `secondary_track` to exactly one `dynamics_link` with a typed driver, response, material, spatial scope, response strength, lag, damping, and settling. Use `propagates_to` only for a real causal chain and keep it acyclic. Body acceleration, contact, gravity, airflow, and object movement are possible drivers. Preserve causality across hair, fabric, soft tissue, accessories, props, and surroundings; avoid independent loops.

## Scene physics and visibility

Record support and object contact by subject and beat, linking contact surfaces to known objects where possible. A grip must precede lifting, a planted foot must support weight transfer, and released objects must continue under plausible gravity and momentum. Record gravity and other force fields with qualitative strength and direction. Record environmental drivers only when observed or requested. Use typed `visual_dependencies` for material changes in occlusion, shadow, reflection, illumination, or camera perspective caused by a beat, force, contact, or environmental driver. Do not invent numerical mass, friction coefficients, hidden object geometry, or precise forces from a single image. Use `unknown` when the evidence does not support a qualitative class.

For close facial shots, prioritize facial and head dynamics; for wider shots, prioritize body articulation, support, clothing, and spatial continuity. If the action reveals an unseen surface or changes viewpoint substantially, preserve known identity and mark unseen details as uncertain. A prompt cannot guarantee identity or physical accuracy; the plan states the intended constraints.

## Identity, viewpoints, and spatial interaction

Define subject identity with region-specific `identity_anchors` linked to the references that actually show them. Preserve those anchors through motion, contact, occlusion, and reappearance. Preserve facial geometry and characteristic appearance while allowing the requested expression and head motion; do not turn identity preservation into a frozen face. Record which views and regions the references cover. If a planned view is unsupported, mark reference coverage insufficient and either obtain another reference or constrain the camera, subject orientation, or reveal.

Use `spatial_plan` to keep camera motion, subject motion, and scene dynamics explicit. Give the camera and each subject their own path, depth, orientation, framing, and timing. A change in screen position is not enough to infer whether the camera moved, the subject moved, or both. Record deliberate occlusion intervals and the identity and state that must be restored on reappearance.

Represent interaction as an ordered affordance plan. For human-object interaction, identify the object and coordinate whole-body approach, reach, hand placement, contact, manipulation, release, recoil, and settling as applicable. For human-human interaction, identify each participant's role and plan initiator, receiver, reciprocal response, shared contact, and separation. For human-environment interaction, tie support and travel to scene surfaces and available space. Omit phases that do not apply, but never skip the contact transition that makes a visible state change physically understandable.

## Research basis

- [Behavior Markup Language 1.0](https://repository.cs.ru.is/attachments/download/843/bml-standard-1.pdf) supplies behavior channels and synchronization points; this schema uses its planning ideas rather than claiming BML compliance.
- [Human Motion Video Generation: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03883) locates this agent at the input and motion-planning stages of the human-video pipeline.
- [Laban/Bartenieff Movement Analysis overview](https://labaninternational.org/scope-of-practice/movement-analysis/movement-analysis-overview/) and [EUROLAB theory](https://www.laban-eurolab.org/lbms/theory) support Body, Space, Shape, and Effort descriptions.
- The [Paul Ekman Group's FACS overview](https://www.paulekman.com/facial-action-coding-system/) defines Action Units as observable facial movement components.
- [Breathing and conversational turns](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25385777/) supports coupling speech and respiration when dialogue is present.
- [Breathing Life into Shape](https://files.is.tue.mpg.de/black/papers/siggraph14_breathing.pdf) supports modeling visible respiration; [natural blink/head coupling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13345164/) shows that blink timing can coordinate with head movement rather than following a fixed clock.
- [Combining Active and Passive Simulations for Secondary Motion](https://jamesobrien.com/papers/Obrien-CAP-2000-08/index.html) motivates causal links from controlled movement and environmental forces to passive responses.
- [X-Dyna (CVPR 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Chang_X-Dyna_Expressive_Dynamic_Human_Image_Animation_CVPR_2025_paper.html) motivates context-aware human and environment dynamics; [DreamActor-M1 (ICCV 2025)](https://www.openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Luo_DreamActor-M1_Holistic_Expressive_and_Robust_Human_Image_Animation_with_Hybrid_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf) motivates separate identity, face, and body control; [IPRO (CVPR 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Shen_Identity-Preserving_Image-to-Video_Generation_via_Reward-Guided_Optimization_CVPR_2026_paper.html) highlights identity drift during expression and motion.
- [DeX-Portrait (CVPR 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Shi_DeX-Portrait_Disentangled_and_Expressive_Portrait_Animation_via_Explicit_and_Latent_CVPR_2026_paper.html) and [FG-Portrait (CVPR 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Xu_FG-Portrait_3D_Flow_Guided_Editable_Portrait_Animation_CVPR_2026_paper.html) motivate separating head pose, expression, and identity rather than treating the face as one rigid signal.
- [PoseAnything (CVPR 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Wang_PoseAnything_General_Pose-guided_Video_Generation_with_Part-aware_Temporal_Coherence_CVPR_2026_paper.html) and [BulletTime (CVPR 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Wang_BulletTime_Decoupled_Control_of_Time_and_Camera_Pose_for_Video_CVPR_2026_paper.html) motivate part-level temporal coherence and explicit separation of camera trajectory from scene motion.
- [Free-viewpoint Human Animation (CVPR 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Hong_Free-viewpoint_Human_Animation_with_Pose-correlated_Reference_Selection_CVPR_2025_paper.html) motivates checking whether references cover the viewpoints that planned motion will reveal.
- [Animate Anyone 2 (ICCV 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Hu_Animate_Anyone_2_High-Fidelity_Character_Image_Animation_with_Environment_Affordance_ICCV_2025_paper.html), [Human-Object Interaction from Human-Level Instructions (ICCV 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Wu_Human-Object_Interaction_from_Human-Level_Instructions_ICCV_2025_paper.html), and [HUMOTO (ICCV 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Lu_HUMOTO_A_4D_Dataset_of_Mocap_Human_Object_Interactions_ICCV_2025_paper.html) motivate explicit affordances, whole-body and hand coordination, contact, and object-aware motion.
- [Ponimator (ICCV 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Liu_Ponimator_Unfolding_Interactive_Pose_for_Versatile_Human-human_Interaction_Animation_ICCV_2025_paper.html) motivates reciprocal reaction planning for close human-human interaction.
